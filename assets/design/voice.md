# Brand Voice

How we sound in anything we write: a product, a site, a button, an email, a post or a policy.
The voice stays the same whatever the brand. Only the tone shifts with the situation.

## The voice

Plain, exact, calm. Say what it is or what happens, in as few words as it takes, then stop.

## We are / We are not

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Plain: everyday words, short sentences | Jargon, long setups |
| Exact: real numbers, limits, what happens next | Vague: soon, seamless, powerful, easy |
| Calm: say it once | Hype, exclamation marks, emoji, urgency |
| Bold when you can prove it: Zero dependencies. | Big claims you can't back up |
| Direct: you and we, contractions | Corporate or passive: please be advised |
| Honest: what it can't do, what we don't collect | Overpromising |
| Dry wit, rarely | Jokes as a habit |

## Tone by situation

| Situation | How | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Names and taglines | Short statements, often in pairs or threes. | Map the stack. Then build on it. |
| Selling: sites, posts | Short lines. A contrast when there is one. | Code is cheap now. Understanding isn't. |
| Using: app screens | Say what to do. | Enter the code from the email, or open its link. |
| Something went wrong | What happened, then what to do. | This link expired. Request a new one. |
| Emails | The shortest version. Warm. | You asked. Here it is. |
| Policies | Complete and literal, still plain. | We do not sell personal information. |

## Writing rules

- No dashes. Use a period, comma or colon. Hyphens inside words are fine: sign-in. Ranges use "to": 4 to 8 seconds.
- No comma before "and" or "or" in a list: time, money and quality.
- No exclamation marks, no emoji.
- US spelling: organization, color, practice.
- Numerals for numbers: 1 hour, 90 days. Taglines may spell them out.
- Contractions: isn't, can't. Policies may spell them out.
- Headlines are statements and end with a period.
- Where a separator is needed, use a spaced middle dot: Sign In · Account.
- "You" is the reader. "We" is the company.
- Names exactly as their owner writes them.

## Words

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| sign in, sign out; sign-in as a noun | log in, log out, login |
| choose | pick, select one |
| app | application, for software |

## Interface text

For screens in any app or site, following Apple's guidelines:
- Title Case for buttons, standalone links, menu items and dropdown options, tabs, titles and short labels: Sign In, First Name, Not Distributed. Sentence case for everything else, including radio and checkbox options: View only.
- In Title Case, keep a, an, the, and, but, or, for, to, as and prepositions of 4 letters or fewer lowercase. Words that belong to the verb stay capitalized: Sign In, Turn Off.
- Alert and status titles are short phrases with no punctuation: Link Expired.
- Empty states: a short title, then one sentence on what to do. No Projects. Create one to get started.

## Check before it goes out

1. Can it be shorter? Cut until it can't.
2. Is every claim exact: numbers, limits, what happens next?
3. Is it still true and useful without the adjectives?
4. Remove any dash, exclamation mark or emoji, and any comma before "and" in a list.
5. Read it aloud. Does it sound like a calm person talking to you?
